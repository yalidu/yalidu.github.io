I am a Senior Lecturer (Associate Professor) in AI at King’s College London, where I head the Distributed AI Group and lead the Cooperative AI Lab. I am also a Turing Fellow and secondee at The Alan Turing Institute, and a member of the European Laboratory for Learning and Intelligent Systems (ELLIS).

My research develops cooperative and safe AI agents that can learn, coordinate, and align with humans in complex decision-making environments, with a focus on scalable multi-agent learning, human–AI coordination, and value alignment.

I received the AAAI New Faculty Highlights Award and was named a Rising Star in AI by KAUST in 2023. I serve as Senior Area Chair for AAMAS and NAACL, Area Chair for NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR and IJCAI, and Associate Editor for IEEE Transactions on Artificial Intelligence, and have served on the organising committees of AAMAS 2023, NeurIPS 2024, DAI 2025 and AAMAS 2027.

Join us. I normally recruit 2–3 PhD students each year. If you are interested in joining the lab, please read the guidance for prospective students before getting in touch.

research: safe and cooperative AI

Three connected directions in the Cooperative AI Lab. The goal: agents that can learn, coordinate and remain safe in multi-agent and human-centred environments.

Evaluating agent societies

What emerges when many agents interact under mixed motives?

  • SocialJax (ICLR '26)
  • OpenSocialJax (arXiv '26)
  • LLM agent populations (AAMAS '25)

Learning & aligning

How can agents coordinate at scale, with partners and with humans?

  • Scalable networked MARL (Nature MI '24, NeurIPS '25)
  • Zero-shot human–AI coordination (NeurIPS '23)
  • Learning from human and language feedback (ICML '25, EMNLP '25)

Safety & oversight

How do we keep agent societies safe and accountable?

  • Delegation safety: BazaarBench (arXiv '26)
  • Safe MARL with language constraints (AAAI '26)
  • Multi-agent AI safety and governance
